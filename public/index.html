New in 0.2.22

Acid Bass.

Windows and Linux 0.2.22.0 add Acid Bass, a monophonic instrument in the Rack. It has eight knobs you can automate: Tune, Cutoff, Resonance, Env, Decay, Accent, Wave and Model. 3C slides. 3D accents. The Mac download is still 0.2.21.0, so it does not include Acid Bass yet. Older songs still open.

New in 0.2.21

One menu, eight places.

0.2.21.0 added an eight-place pie menu. It follows what you clicked: a track, a clip, an empty lane, a Rack instrument or effect, an automation lane, or Song / Master. Save, Save As… and Export… are on the song title and on Song / Master. The Mac download is this version. Windows and Linux 0.2.22.0 include these menus too. Songs still open, play and render exactly as in 0.2.20.0.

0.2.21

Eight places, fixed

Right-click and the places stay put: make, repeat, edit, sound, time, look, hear, remove. An empty place stays empty, so the others do not slide. A choice marked › replaces the ring, and Back is always the lower left.

Where you clicked

The Rack and the lanes

The ring names the thing you clicked and does not change the selection until you choose. Esc, or a click in the middle, cancels. On a Rack instrument the effect ring offers EQ, Compressor, Delay, Reverb, Filter and Chorus. An effect's Add lane lists that effect's knobs.

Save & Export

Easier to find

Click the song title for Save, Save As… and Export…. Right-click Song / Master for Master…, Export… and Save. Remove… asks again: the first click only opens the confirm ring, and Delete is the second click.

In the Mac 0.2.21.0 download, and in Windows and Linux 0.2.22.0. Shortcuts, the list menus and the chord pie are unchanged.

New in 0.2.20

Text that fits, and modulation you can see.

Windows and Linux 0.2.22.0 include this, and so does the Mac 0.2.21.0 download. It fixes text that overlapped or was cut off, starting with the Arp card's header in the Rack, and makes the modulation markers on knobs easy to see in every theme and knob style. Songs open, play and render exactly as in 0.2.19.0.

The Arp

A header that fits

In the Rack at 1366×768 and narrower, Simple and Steps were cut off under Preset ▾ and More. The card now measures its header in the real font, Text size and UI scale, so nothing overlaps and the text stays full size.

Everywhere

Names that end in “…”

Timeline markers on the same beat share one label, and marker, clip and scene names end in “…” instead of being cut mid-letter. Automation scale labels, the Arranger's role badge, the Browser hint, the FM picture and the instrument cards' choice rows all fit too.

Modulation

Markers you can see

The M1–M8 badges and the slot dot on knobs use the theme's accent colour at a contrast of at least 4.5:1. The dot is filled once a modulation is assigned, and the rings and depth bars are thicker and brighter in all six knob styles.

New in 0.2.19

A top bar you can rearrange, The Matrix and six knob styles.

Top bar

Drag the panels

The top bar is three panels: the Display, the Transport and the Master. Drag one by its grip to put them in the order you like; the middle one sits in the centre of the window. Settings › Look › Top bar shows the order and can reset it.

Theme

The Matrix

Phosphor green on near-black, in Settings › Look. Clips keep their track colours, so a busy arrangement stays easy to read.

Knobs

Six knob styles

Pick how every knob in the app looks: Classic (each theme's own knob), Flat ring, Chicken-head, LED ring, Brushed metal or Pixel. Each choice shows a small live knob in the current theme's colours before you click.

Still new from 0.2.18: the Arp, a note effect that plays the notes you hold with a step grid of up to 32 steps; a loop range you drag by either end; and Ctrl+drag to copy clips while the originals stay put. All of it is in the changelog and the manual.

The studio

Everything a song needs, in one window.

Arrange on a timeline, write notes, program drums, shape the sound and mix it, with no project files to fight.

Arrange

Timeline and clip launcher

Drag clips across tracks, loop one clip with a double-click, split, copy and paste. Scenes in the Clip Arranger launch on the next bar, so you can try a whole arrangement without leaving the window.

  • Up to 32 tracks per song, each with its own colour and role
  • Loop, follow actions and Capture turn a jam into a timeline
  • Automation lanes for filters, levels, delay feedback and any knob

Write

Piano roll, drums and honest chords

Draw notes with velocity, pressure, chance and step conditions. The Drum Kit's step sequencer has swing, 16 to 64 steps and a groove generator, and the chord helper writes real notes to the timeline — one Ctrl+Z and it's yours.

  • Round Robin: clip variations that differ each pass but render the same
  • Chords, Melody and Bass generators with a WRITE card that says where the notes go
  • Scale lock for typing and MIDI, with key detection

Sound

A rack for every track

The Rack is the instrument and its effects in one row under the Timeline. Stack layers, split the keyboard into parallel paths, nest devices, save the result as your own rack and pull it back from the Browser.

  • Add Device from a tree, drag to reorder, Ctrl-drag to copy
  • Splits and layers with level, pan, transpose and key range
  • Save Custom Rack, then find it in Browser › My Racks

Mix

Mixer, ducking and a true-peak limiter

Per-channel fader, pan, mute, solo and peak meters, two send/return buses, and sidechain ducking with a Duck % per channel for the classic trance pump. The master limiter is a lookahead true-peak design, and the loudness meter reads LUFS the way the standards do.

  • Momentary, short-term and integrated LUFS plus true peak (ITU-R BS.1770)
  • Every export goes through the same limiter
  • WAV and MP3 with ID3 tags, whole song or one block

Classic

The pattern editor is still there

For the old way of working: OctaMED-style blocks, note and effect columns, the Z–M keyboard, selection, transpose and undo. Effects cover slides, decays, tempo, filter and acid lines; the cursor follows the playing row.

  • 64-row blocks by default, any length you like, with named Undo steps
  • 32 channels with a bar that says which ones you are looking at
  • 303 glide and accent (3C / 3D), plus Voice and generator effects

Find

A Browser that knows everything

Instruments, CLAP and VST3 plugins, samples, songs, presets, Grid patches and wavetables in one searchable panel with tags, filters, favourites and ratings. Drag a sound onto a track header to give that track the sound, or below the last track to make a new one.

  • Find plugins on the first run, or any time in Settings
  • Double-click to load, drag to assign, audition as you go
  • Chop and slice a break into instruments, or straight into notes

Plugins

CLAP and VST3, hosted properly

Surge XT and other CLAP instruments play from the timeline like any other instrument. Eight macro knobs are there for automation and 43xy, every plugin parameter is searchable, and the plugin state is saved inside the song.

  • Crash-guarded scanner, so one bad plugin does not take the app down
  • Native editor on Windows, offline render for the whole song
  • MIDI keyboards with velocity, sustain, pitch bend and MIDI learn

The sounds

Synths with character, made from scratch.

Every instrument is written for ChipWave itself: oscillators with drift, a Moog-style ladder, formant voices, FM and a pile of retro game noise. All of them are editable knob by knob, and all of them are in the free download.

Acid Bass Mono bass

A monophonic Rack instrument in Windows and Linux 0.2.22.0. Eight knobs. 3C slides and 3D accents. The voice is from OpenRB-338 by pingw33n and the OpenRB-338 authors, GPL-3.0-only. Not in the Mac 0.2.21.0 download.

WaveForge Synth

Two band-limited oscillators morphing pulse → saw → octave saw, up to 16 unison voices each, six blend modes, four filter types and eight mod routes.

StringWave Strings

A string ensemble ported in from its author's own synth: layered string machines with slow drift and a warm stereo spread.

Analog Synth

A Moog-style synth from scratch: three drifting oscillators, PWM, noise, the vintage ladder filter, filter and amp envelopes, with Fat Moog Bass and Acid Squelch presets.

FM-4 FM synth

Four operators, the eight DX21/TX81Z algorithms, feedback and an envelope per operator. E-Piano, Metallic Bell, Psy Zap Bass and Growl are ready to play.

Drum Kit & Drum Synth Beats

32 pads, 13 kits, choke groups, a step sequencer with velocity and swing, and a macro drum synth with Dice, Mutate and Bake to sample.

Chip FX Retro

A port of sfxr: pickups, lasers, explosions, power-ups, hits, jumps and blips that play in tune with the pattern note.

Voice Vocal

No samples: a glottal pulse through formant filters, vowels you build from chips, babble, stutter, spoken words, Throat mode and a metallic/digital/sub Transform.

Noise Texture

White, pink, brown and crackle through a ladder or state-variable filter with a sweep envelope. Risers, downlifters, impacts and vinyl.

Wave & Grid Wavetable

Wavetable and Grid patch instruments for when you want a sound that is not a subtractive synth at all.

Sampler Samples

Import WAV by button or drag and drop, loop, preview, and chop a break at its transients into slices or straight into notes.

Effects Mix

Filter, chorus/flanger, phaser, frequency shifter, delay, reverb, drive, bitcrusher, compressor, metal resonator, digital distortion and sub bass.

Plugins CLAP / VST3

Host CLAP and VST3 instruments and effects alongside the built-in ones, with macros, automation and saved plugin state.

Four looks

Slate, Dark Circuit, The Matrix, or a workbench from 1986.

Four themes, switched in View › Theme or Settings › Look. Slate is the default — the Ember skin on slate grey. Dark Circuit is the night-time studio. The Matrix is phosphor green on near-black. Amiga is the light Workbench look with blue headers and an orange cursor, for people who miss their A500.

Every theme keeps the same layout, keys and shortcuts, so switching never moves anything. Settings › Look also has six knob styles that work with every theme, a UI scale in 25 % steps (Ctrl+= / Ctrl+-) and a text size setting for tired eyes and small laptops.

Linux

0.2.22.0

x86_64 only, glibc 2.35 or newer, built on Ubuntu 22.04. Unsigned. No Flatpak, Snap or ARM build. Includes Acid Bass.

.deb · 13.0 MB AppImage · 16.3 MB .rpm · 16.4 MB tar.gz · 16.0 MB

Debian, Ubuntu and Mint: sudo apt install ./chipwave-studio_0.2.22.0_amd64.deb. That .deb was installed, self-tested and removed on Ubuntu 22.04. The .rpm installs with sudo dnf install ./chipwave-studio-0.2.22.0-1.x86_64.rpm or sudo zypper install ./chipwave-studio-0.2.22.0-1.x86_64.rpm. Remove with sudo apt remove chipwave-studio, sudo dnf remove chipwave-studio or sudo zypper remove chipwave-studio. Songs and settings in your home directory stay. The Linux binary's self-test passed on the build host.

The AppImage uses the system ALSA, OpenGL and X11 or Wayland libraries. It does not bundle glibc. chmod +x ChipWave-0.2.22.0-linux-x86_64.AppImage and run it. A direct launch has to mount the file, and that mount cannot run without FUSE: install FUSE (fuse3 and libfuse2), or run --appimage-extract and then squashfs-root/AppRun. Unpack the tar.gz and run ./chipwave. ARM and older glibc still build from source.

On 0.2.3.5 or later, Help › Check for updates… follows GitHub's Latest release, now 0.2.22.0, which has the Windows and Linux files. It does not include a new Mac package. The app opens with a clean song: File › Demo songs, then Space.
